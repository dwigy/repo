# [GAME] world canon

Region names, tellings and terms live in `js/lexicon.js`. This document inlines their current values.

## What everyone knows

Long ago, every force of the universe lived in harmony. That time has no name, and never gets one. Children draw it as a circle of colours. Grandparents say it was quieter than that.

Then the Great Split. A legend as old as time, told big, told differently every time. A battle in the sky, or something like one. Every force came apart into fragments. They fell across the universe the way rain falls. Nobody knows why. Nobody knows how many. New fragments are found every year. They always will be.

## Fragments and companions

A fragment is not an object. It is more like weather. A mood, a warmth, living where it fell. Some rivers are brave. Some kitchens are kind.

When a person and a fragment meet, there is a pull. Immediate, natural, emotional. Together they recreate, in a small way, the harmony from before.

So nobody puts a fragment to work. People have companions. You carry one, meet one, play one, find one.

## Forms

When a fragment settles with a person, it takes a form. A creature with too many feet. A stone with a face. A wisp in the corner.

Forms are a fixed catalogue. The bond is individual. Two companions of the same form are the same kind. They are different friends.

Every companion has a name the player gives it. A record of where and when it was found. Quirks, which show on the board.

## Discs and the Tinker

Centuries ago, someone gave fragments somewhere to live: a small blank disc. Nobody knows who. The world calls them the Tinker. It loves them like a grandparent never met.

Tinker's Night is the longest night of the year. Lanterns. Discs out on every table. Everyone's companions out at once. Stories that get taller every year.[^1]

[^1]: Some devotional folks leave a disc on the windowsill for the Tinker. The tone is still a party.

## The Dimming

Fragments once changed the weather. Over centuries their power faded. Now a fragment is a nudge, a warmth, a friend. The world calls this the Dimming. Nobody knows if it is a loss or a peace. It is why companions are collected and shared, not guarded.

## Blanks, shops and the Breath

Shops sell blanks: empty discs, hand-made in small batches. Blanks come sealed, a few to a pack.

There is a rumour, apparently true. A blank stays empty until it is opened. The opener's own breath draws a fragment in and gives it form. People call this the Breath. It is why pulls feel like yours. And why a pack a friend opened never feels right.

## Light (rarity)

The same form can settle more than once. Each keeps a different amount of the fragment's original light.

- **Common.** A faint echo.
- **Uncommon.** A glimmer.
- **Rare.** A steady glow.
- **Mythic.** Bright enough to read by.
- **Legendary.** Kept most of its light.
- **Whole fragment (1/1).** The only unbroken pieces. Each is held by a Keeper.

## The stack

A stack is twenty companions a person chose to carry together. A small portrait of the person and their friendships. Twenty carried, twelve played.

Asking to see someone's stack is a personal question.

## The game

The game is the world's biggest sport and oldest form of respect.

The board has twelve sockets: six in front, six in back. The front row faces the other player. The back row holds it up.

A full board is a full day. Six sockets of daylight, six of night.

A match is called a Meeting. Players bow before and after. The bow is the line. On one side, the people who play. On the other, the people who only take.

## Students and champions

Anyone can play. Most play in their town. Some go further: to collect, to play, to know the universe personally. Those are students.

A champion is what a student becomes. Very few do. Most stop somewhere. No shame in it.

## The seven regions

Seven regions, in order, climbing. A region is a town and the country around it. Each has a shop, three players, places to explore, one Keeper. At the Summit the light turns white.

| 1. The Hearth | a warm village with root cellars and one long main street | red | Foundation | Stand somewhere before you go anywhere. | Where do you come from? |
| 2. The Tide | a river town of docks and ferries | orange | Flow | Let things move. Feel what you feel. | What do you want? |
| 3. The Forge | a workshop town of kilns and sun-baked terraces | yellow | Will | Make something and stand behind it. | What will you do? |
| 4. The Grove | an orchard town with a wind that smells green | green | Care | Play for the other player too. | Who is this for? |

| 6. The Observatory | a hill town under clear cold nights | indigo | Sight | See the board that is really there. | What do you see? |
| 7. The Summit | a handful of buildings above the weather | purple, then white | Wholeness | Bring it all with you. | Who are you now? |

## The Keepers

A Keeper is not a gate. A Keeper knows the lesson best and has waited to teach it. They cannot be flattered or forced. They are beaten only in a respectful, balanced Meeting.

A true win earns their telling, with one story only they hold. A Seal. The region's whole fragment.

1. **The Hearth.** A baker with flour on everything. Asks about your family before every match. Telling: Luck.
2. **The Tide.** A ferry pilot who never stops moving and never seems rushed. Telling: Seed.
3. **The Forge.** A young smith with burnt eyebrows and an opinion about everything. No telling picked yet, and says so.
4. **The Grove.** An old beekeeper who moves slowly and never gets stung. Plays like the board is a garden. Telling: Love.
5. **The Choir.** A retired singer who talks so quietly you have to listen. Telling: Mirror.
6. **The Observatory.** An astronomer who plays with the lights off. Knows your stack before you show it. Telling: Sleep, gently.
7. **The Summit.** Barely there. The fewest words in the game. Holds all the tellings. Will not say which is true.

## The five tellings

A telling is a belief about the Split. The five cards, verbatim:

- **The Love telling.** "Harmony broke itself on purpose. So it could be found again, piece by piece, by hands that chose it."
- **The Luck telling.** "Nothing chose anything. Chance is the oldest law. The fragments are lucky because they remember the roll."
- **The Seed telling.** "Harmony did not break. It planted itself. A seed comes apart to grow."
- **The Mirror telling.** "Harmony looked at itself and saw two. The fragments are the one meeting itself."
- **The Sleep telling.** "Harmony is not gone. It is asleep. The fragments are its dreams."

The tellings are never resolved. No line in the game confirms or denies one.

## The Hall

Seven Seals earn an invitation. The Hall is the world's circle of champions. It is benevolent and rules nothing. It teaches, it repairs, it shows up when something is wrong.

Twelve seats, and a thirteenth chair, always empty. Nobody remembers who it is for.

Three of the twelve test each arrival. A student who passes is offered the empty chair. The game never explains why.

## Completion

Completion means every known companion. Nobody has done it. The catalogue is open-ended: say "known", never "total".

The reward is a poster. The player's name, in the largest type in the app. Nothing else.

## [CORP]

[CORP] is a corporation. It collects for the wrong reason: to own the universe. It cannot pass a Keeper, so it buys whole collections. It opens shops selling "better" blanks. It sponsors things. It convinces towns the old game is for children. Never loud. Convincing.

[CORP] believes the Dimming was a theft. Its plan is the Gathering: every fragment in one building, one hand.

Its faces: the Chief, unseen until the end, and ordinary once seen. Three lieutenants mirror three Keepers. A false baker, a false pilot, a false astronomer.

The arc. Region 2: a friendly buyer offers too much for one companion. Region 4: a shop is emptied. Region 6: [CORP] in the open, holding a stolen whole fragment. Foot of region 7: the Gathering begins. It halts everyone until it is stopped.

[CORP] is beaten by being out-played. In a Meeting it did not want. In the open. With a bow.

## The ending

After the Hall's three, the player is asked one question. Which telling do you believe? There is no wrong answer.

## Appendix A: Rules for writers

- Never explain the Split. Nobody knows why.
- Never count fragments.
- Companions are not tools. Carry, meet, play, bring, find.
- Cards are twelve words or fewer. Present tense. Warm, plain, cute. Wit over explanation.
- No proper names for people. Say "the baker", "a kid with a kite".
- Allowed proper nouns: the Tinker, Tinker's Night, the Breath, the Dimming, the Great Split, the Hall, the Gathering, the Chief, region names, telling names, [GAME], [CORP].
- The time before the Split has no name.
- Placeholders pending: [GAME], [CORP], the brand word (`TERM.brand` in the lexicon).
